## 0. The one-paragraph version

Deep-Dream's two design commitments have precise information-theoretic translations.
At **write time**, entity alignment is treated as a *deferred decision under
accumulating evidence*: observations are append-only and linked to source spans, so
alignment errors are revisable coarsenings, not information loss — under an explicit
i.i.d. feature model, the probability that a true merge stays undetected decays
exponentially in pooled evidence (Chernoff information), which is the formal shape of
"entities converge as information accumulates." At **read time**, the derived layer is
an *index, not a reconstruction*: every rung's answerer consumes verbatim spans, so the
derived layer can lose addressing information but never reconstruction fidelity
(reconstruction never happens); the rate–distortion operating point is chosen per
query by the agent's reading policy rather than fixed at write time by a compressor;
and the evidence gate makes addressing errors one-sided — they can change *what gets
read*, never the authenticity or auditability of *what is cited*.

## 1. Formal setup

Let the source stream be $S = D_1, \dots, D_T$ — immutable, authoritative documents.
Ingestion produces mentions with feature vectors
$X_t \in \mathcal{X}$ (name embeddings, content embeddings, attribute assertions,
relation-endpoint co-occurrence patterns) and each mention is linked to the exact
source span it came from. Let $Z$ denote the ground-truth partition of mentions into
real-world entities. The memory's family state $F_t$ is an online clustering of
mentions seen so far; each family $f$ carries an append-only observation list
$O_f = \{(o_j, \text{span}_j)\}$.

Two implementation facts anchor everything below (file:line in this repository):

- **Append-only with non-destructive merge.** Merging families re-attaches the
  loser's observations/mentions under the winner with history preserved, and is
  idempotent under a redirect registry (`core/storage/sqlite/merge.py:209`;
  `core/remember/align_v2.py:77-111`, "redirect 语义, 幂等"). Nothing an
  observation ever asserted is deleted by a merge.
- **Conservative alignment thresholds.** Low-confidence merge verdicts degrade to
  `create_new` (batch confidence < 0.75, or < 0.9 in conservative mode —
  `core/remember/entity_alignment.py:331-347`); LLM `uncertain` verdicts create a new
  family (`core/remember/entity_candidates.py:414-454`). The system deliberately
  over-splits under weak evidence.

At read time, an agent interacts with tools; a tool call emits (candidate set,
verbatim span text). The evidence gate admits into the final answer only identifiers
surfaced by a tool call in the current episode, and answers cite spans
(`sections/3_method.tex`, gate contract; deterministic enforcement verified in §4.4).

## 2. Write time: deferred decisions under accumulating evidence

### Prop 1 (Revisability — what append-only buys)

*Claim.* In the append-only data model, any coarsening of the current family
partition is realizable at any later time by a sequence of non-destructive merges,
with no loss of evidence; merges are idempotent, so repeated convergence passes
converge to a fixed point.

*Proof sketch.* A merge of families $a, b$ replaces them with a family whose
observation set is $O_a \cup O_b$ (loser rows re-attached, provenance preserved —
implementation fact above). Coarsenings are generated by such unions; the union is
associative and commutative, and the redirect registry makes re-merging an already
merged family a no-op. $\square$

*Why it matters.* The alternative architecture — overwrite the entity record with a
running summary $\sigma(f)$ — implements a many-to-one compression at write time. By
the data processing inequality, $I(Z; \sigma) \le I(Z; O_f)$, with strict inequality
whenever the summary discards identity-discriminative detail; the discarded
likelihood terms cannot be restored by any later evidence. **First-shot alignment
accuracy is not the quantity that matters; evidence retention is.** The system is
correct to be sloppy early and converge late, provided it never throws away the
inputs to the decision.

### Prop 2 (Convergence rate under pooled evidence)

*Assumptions (explicit, idealized).* (A1) Conditional on entity identity $e$,
observation features are i.i.d. draws from $P_e$. (A2) For distinct entities
$e \neq e'$, $P_e \neq P_{e'}$ with Chernoff information
$C(P_e, P_{e'}) = -\min_{0 \le \lambda \le 1} \log \int p_e^{\lambda}
p_{e'}^{1-\lambda} \, d\mu > 0$.

*Claim.* Treat the merge decision for two families (pooled $k = |O_a| + |O_b|$
observations) as testing $H_0$: single entity vs $H_1$: two entities. The Bayesian
error of the likelihood-ratio test decays as
$\Pr[\text{error at } k \text{ observations}] \le \exp(-k \, C(P_e, P_{e'}))$
\citep{chernoff1952measure}. Consequently: the probability that a *true* merge —
split early under weak evidence — remains undetected decays exponentially in
corroborating observations, independent of how wrong the initial segmentation was.

*System correspondence.*
- The window-batch adjudication pools an entire window's candidates into one LLM
  call (`entity_alignment.py:566-617`), and the scope-end sweep re-judges same-name
  groups and substring-alias pairs with full-library context, iterating to a fixed
  point (≤ 3 rounds) because merges cascade new evidence (`align_v2.py:203-278`).
  Both are exactly "re-test with more pooled evidence": they raise $k$ per decision
  rather than trusting the first test.
- Measured anchors (evidence ledger, engine_comparison / library_dbs, schema v5):
  same-name duplicate families 340 → 124 on MAB scopes and 482 → 99 on LME;
  duplicate-name groups 154 → 61 (MAB); while ingest LLM calls per document fall
  46–65%. Batching converts per-mention decisions into per-cohort decisions: more
  evidence per decision, fewer decisions — consolidation quality and ingest
  efficiency improve together, which is what the pooled-evidence view predicts.

*Caveats, stated.* A1–A2 are idealizations: real features are correlated across
windows, extraction noise is heavy-tailed, and same-name-different-entity pairs are
precisely cases where $P_e, P_{e'}$ overlap (low $C$) — the residual 124/99 same-name
groups are plausibly mostly *true* distinct entities, and same-name duplicate rows
are a proxy for residual duplication, not a bound (unlinked aliases can also remain).
The measurements span two engine generations, not a controlled sweep of $k$; the
exponential rate is a model, not a measurement.

### Remark (the error asymmetry is a designed choice, and it is the *right* side)

The thresholds bias errors toward over-splitting. Prop 1 makes over-splits
recoverable (merge later when evidence arrives); Prop 3 below makes their read-time
cost one-sided (they cost retrieval attention, not answer authenticity). A wrong
*merge*, by contrast, mixes two entities' evidence under one handle — an addressing
*contamination* that is strictly harder for the read path to detect (everything it
retrieves is real text). Choosing the recoverable error mode under uncertainty is
the design's answer to the no-free-lunch of early alignment.

### Remark (relations as discriminative features and as addressing paths)

Relation-endpoint co-occurrence is injected into the alignment context
(`core/remember/entity.py:744-760`; batch adjudication can emit `relations_to_create`
that link a new entity to a candidate — `entity_alignment.py:391-404`). In testing
terms, shared relational neighborhoods are high-Chernoff features: the likelihood
ratio of "same entity" vs "different" under matching relation-endpoint patterns is
far from 1. The same edges later widen the retrieval graph (RELATES-neighbor
expansion in `core/storage/sqlite/graph_traversal.py`). One link structure serves
both the write-time test and the read-time index — which is the formal content of
"通过概念实体、关系丰富链接".

## 3. The store: an index, not a reconstruction

Define two loss types for a derived memory layer over sources $S$:

- **Reconstruction loss** — the distortion between what the answerer reads and the
  source text it purportedly represents.
- **Addressing loss** — the probability/gap structure of failing to *find* the
  relevant source span.

Deep-Dream's derived layer (families, relations, observations) is never the answer
text: all three rungs' answerers consume verbatim spans — rung 1's payload is top-$k$
*source spans* from a fused retrieval call; rung 2's answerer reads submitted
evidence resolved to sources; rung 3 reads sandboxed documents (retrieval resolves
candidates back to `episode_id` → source snippets — `library_manager.py:1341-1357,
2275-2285`). So reconstruction distortion is structurally zero (undefined, really —
reconstruction never occurs); all loss is addressing loss. This is the information-
theoretic identity of "let verbatim sources authorize answers": **the derived layer
may lose information about how to find a fact, never about the fact itself.**

Complexity note (structural, not an empirical superiority claim): candidate
generation runs over the family index (FTS5 over episodes/observations, embedding
dot products over observation/relation tables, BFS over concept graphs, reciprocal-
rank fusion $w/(60+\text{rank}+1)$) — cost scales with index size, not source bytes;
the macro→micro path (concept handle → family → observations → span) is what makes
"宏观快速概念检索 + 索引到真实原文" one deterministic resolution chain rather than
two subsystems.

## 4. Read time: query-chosen rate–distortion, one-sided error

### Prop 3 (One-sided addressing error — the gate)

*Claim.* The answer is a functional of (question, spans actually read), and the gate
admits only identifiers surfaced by tool calls in the current episode. A perturbation
of the derived layer (alignment error, stale relation, wrong scope materialization)
enters this functional *only* through the candidate sets tools emit — it can change
what is read (coverage), never the authenticity of what is cited: cited spans remain
verbatim source spans with intact provenance chains, for any overlay state.

*Proof sketch.* Direct from the gate definition and the immutability of $S$: tool
outputs = (candidates ∝ overlay, text ∝ source); the gate checks identifier
provenance, and span text is a function of the source store alone. $\square$

This formalizes the paper's standing sentence — an overlay error can cause a missed
scope but never a fabricated fact — and keeps its existing scope: the gate enforces
*provenance reachability*, not semantic entailment (§3.6). The one-sidedness is at
the addressing layer; support remains human-auditable either way.

### Prop 4 (Deferred rate–distortion selection — progressive disclosure)

*Claim (structural).* A summarizing memory system fixes a rate–distortion point
$(R_0, D_0)$ at write time, and every future query inherits it. A source-grounded
system fixes no such point: the answerer's context $C(q)$ is chosen per query by the
reading policy $\rho$ over an index that resolves to verbatim spans — the
rate–distortion choice is deferred from producer to consumer.

*Measured operating points (ledger, rung_cost_v2 + tracks; systems-comparison
wording per contract item 24).* Three reading policies over the same frozen
libraries and question stream, v2 engine: (25.1k tokens, 0.324) → (35.0k, 0.407) →
(96.0k, 0.651) Overall, replicated v1 (0.3025/0.3759/0.6695); the consolidation
tasks separate hardest (FC-MH 2–4 vs 67–70), consistent with tasks whose required
evidence set is not one-shot-retrievable but is sequentially locatable. The X7
channel ablation bounds the *offline addressing* contribution of the derived
channels at ±2.4 pp (equivalence bound, dev slice) — the derived layer's measured
value is selection/navigation (which sources to read), exercised in rungs 2–3, not
offline recall.

*Autonomy, formally.* The tools expose a proposal distribution (candidates per
channel, fused); the agent owns the decision policy (what to read, when to stop,
what to verify). Sequential reading is adaptive information acquisition — each read
reshapes the next query — whereas a fixed payload is one-shot compression chosen
before seeing the question's difficulty. The ladder's monotone token-accuracy
frontier is the measured trace of agents choosing their own operating points; the
paper claims the association, not single-factor causality.

## 5. What this framing does NOT claim

1. No claim that derived channels improve offline recall — X7 says they do not
   (±2.4 pp equivalence bound) on the development slice; wording stays at
   "non-authoritative navigation".
2. No causal isolation claim for the ladder (contract item 24 wording stands).
3. The exponential convergence rate is conditional on A1–A2 and is not measured as
   a rate; the measured facts are the two-generation duplicate-family reductions
   and per-document call reductions.
4. No complexity or latency superiority claim over any external system — the
   index-vs-scan note is structural.
5. Same-name duplicate statistics are a proxy for residual duplication, not a bound.
