\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup and caliber discipline}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{MemoryAgentBench (MAB)} \citep{hu2025memoryagentbench} is the paper's primary instrument because its official scorer and published per-domain tables permit a cross-system comparison under one protocol. We run a stratified sampled subset: 767 questions over 10 scopes and 59 documents, against the benchmark's full 3{,}671 questions over 29 tasks. The subset covers all four competencies but not every task (task lists in \cref{app:sampling}); all MAB numbers below carry this subset statement, with a composition-adjusted Overall beside any Overall figure (\cref{sec:cross-system}). Scoring uses the official MemoryAgentBench scorer at commit 455306d with an identical \texttt{entity2id} fingerprint across runs; kimi-k3 is the answerer (and the LME-domain judge; the official protocol likewise uses an LLM judge on that domain) for all three tracks, and the ingest model for all libraries. The MAB source corpora are public; we cannot rule out pretraining contact and flag this wherever cross-system numbers lead.

\textbf{LongMemEval-S (LME)} full ingest covers 1{,}176 documents over 25 scopes with per-scope libraries. Track accuracies use paired same-question sets of $n{=}25$ (baseline, skill-agent) and $n{=}27$ (sandbox; two extra questions under its session-evidence eligibility rule), so cross-track comparisons are same-track $v1{\to}v2$ only; we label these small $n$ wherever they appear and treat them as ancillary. The 500-question LME-S run (84.60\%, Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge) is reported separately and protocol-labeled; LoCoMo-1540 under Mem0's exact binary-$J$ protocol and MEME are context and limitations respectively. BigCodeBench (0.4859/0.2838 completion/hard pass@1, calibrated) \citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench} and ALFWorld (0.9786/0.9851 id/ood) \citep{shridhar2021alfworld} are memory-insensitive agent-carrier anchors (appendix).

\subsection{The answer-path ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

\Cref{tab:track-ladder} and \cref{fig:ladder} give the headline result. Holding engine, model, frozen libraries, question stream, and scorer fixed, accuracy rises monotonically with source access at every rung---v2 Overall 0.3240/0.4068/0.6511, v1 0.3025/0.3759/0.6695---and the ladder replicates across two independent engine generations ($n{=}767$ per rung per generation). Paired bootstrap over the 767 shared questions puts the rung deltas at \LadderDeltaSkillBaseCI{} (rung~2 $-$ rung~1) and \LadderDeltaPiSkillCI{} (rung~3 $-$ rung~2). Per-rung compute, recomputed from run logs, is \RungOneCost, \RungTwoCost, and \RungThreeCost{} per question (\cref{fig:cost}): the top rung buys its accuracy with an agentic verification loop.

Two disclosures bound the reading. First, the rungs differ as \emph{systems} (\cref{tab:rung-differences}), so the ladder's deltas are consistent with source verification carrying quality and upper-bound its marginal effect---not a single-factor estimate; the single-factor evidence appears in \cref{sec:provenance-ablation}. Second, rung~1 is not a strawman: the official BM25 row (simple RAG, GPT-4o-mini, full set) scores 41.5 Overall---above our rung~1's 32.4, a disclosed cross-model, cross-subset difference---and collapses on FactConsolidation-MH (3.0, at 262K-average contexts) while our rung~1 collapses on the 6K variant (2--4); a single retrieval shot does not crack consolidation at either length, the verification loop does.

The LME full-ingest tracks echo the ordering at different magnitudes: 0.72/0.72/0.926 (baseline/skill/sandbox, $n{=}25/25/27$, $v2$ engine)---skill and baseline tie in both generations while the sandbox path, on its paired set with a differing eligibility rule (\cref{sec:setup}), scores 0.926 in $v2$ and 0.889 in $v1$, consistent with the evidence path carrying the quality.

\subsection{Cross-system positioning under the official scorer}
\label{sec:cross-system}

\Cref{tab:mab-cross-system} places the sandbox track (top rung; lower rungs in \cref{tab:track-ladder}) beside the official paper's Table~2 rows, which we cite verbatim rather than re-run \citep{hu2025memoryagentbench}. On the scorer's judge-free domains---substring match or fixed accuracy, immune to judge-model identity---the sandbox track exceeds every published row on the length-matched cells (SH-QA 92 vs.\ best published 87; DetectiveQA 83.3 vs.\ 77.5) and posts larger raw margins on EventQA (98 vs.\ 82.6; sampled at 64K--full-book scopes against their 534K full-book average) and FactConsolidation (SH 90 vs.\ 60; MH 70 vs.\ best published 7; our 6K variants against their 262K-average contexts)---mismatched-length cells we read as directional, with the FC accounting below and full scope lengths in \cref{app:sampling}. The composition-adjusted Overall is 59--61 against a best published 49.6 (derivation in \cref{app:adjusted-overall}); we never subtract across calibers---rows differ in answerer, judge, and subset, and the table states each.

\textbf{The model-substitution question.} Our rows use kimi-k3 where published rows use OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic models; a skeptic can ask whether a stronger model, not the architecture, explains the margin. No same-model full-context anchor exists (the kimi-k3 endpoint was retired mid-project; all results are frozen per-item artifacts)---stated as a limitation. Four published facts bound the alternative explanation. (i) Five full-context models across three families---GPT-4o (48.8), GPT-4o-mini (42.2), GPT-4.1-mini (46.9), Gemini-2.0-Flash (42.4), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (49.6)---all fail FactConsolidation-MH ($\le 5$, at 262K-average contexts) and none exceeds 49.6 Overall. (ii) The official backbone ablation (their Table~3) finds that once a backbone is sufficiently strong it is not the bottleneck for retrieval-path agents. (iii) The profile is asymmetric the way an architecture effect predicts and a model effect does not: we \emph{trail} the best published full-context row on the length-matched TTL (46 vs.\ 53.9) and LRU (41.7 vs.\ 62.2) yet lead on AR (92.8 vs.\ 71.8; SH-QA 92 vs.\ 87 is the cleanest matched cell) exactly along the benchmark's own taxonomy; task-matched imputations for the omitted tasks (AR $\approx$84.6, TTL $\approx$29.5, \cref{app:adjusted-overall}) leave the asymmetry intact under uniform model inflation. (iv) The SF leg needs length accounting: the official length-validation (their Table~4) shows o4-mini at 80 on the 6K FactConsolidation-MH variant but 14 at 32K---published FC failure is long-context interference, not capability---so the raw FC juxtaposition above is inflated by the mismatch in our favor, and we claim only the rung separation on identical scopes. Cross-model asymmetry is a property of the official table's construction---its memory rows run GPT-4o-mini against frontier full-context models---not something our row introduces.

As protocol-matched context, LoCoMo-1540 under Mem0's exact binary-$J$ protocol with kimi-k3 as both answerer and judge scores 95.19 (1{,}466/1{,}540, single run), 94.22 under an independent Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge on the same answers, 98.51\% cross-judge agreement (\cref{tab:protocol-other} in \cref{sec:published-baselines}). The two admitted differences from Mem0's published $J$ are a single run versus their 10-run mean$\pm$std and the model substitution, and the run carries a disclosed legacy-fingerprint caveat---reported as protocol-matched context, never a superiority claim.

\subsection{Paired engine comparison: what navigation costs and keeps}
\label{sec:engine-paired}

The $v1{\to}v2$ engine upgrade (cluster convergence atop window-batch alignment) is evaluated as paired runs on identical inputs. Ingest efficiency: LME calls/doc $63.8{\to}22.0$ ($-65.5\%$), tokens/doc $139.0\text{k}{\to}111.7\text{k}$ ($-19.6\%$); MAB calls/doc $609{\to}329$ ($-46\%$), tokens $-18\%$ (45-document paired intersection; manifest gaps in \cref{app:manifest-gaps}). Consolidation quality is maintained: the two target domains move $+0.03$ (TTL MCC $0.43{\to}0.46$, FC-MH $0.67{\to}0.70$; paired CIs \ConsolTTLCI{} and \ConsolFCMHCI{})---within-sample noise we do not interpret as gains; same-name duplicate families fall from 340 to 124 on MAB scopes and from 482 to 99 on LME (recomputed from the per-scope library databases, \cref{app:ledger}). The $v2$ Overall on MAB ($0.6511$) sits $0.018$ below $v1$ ($0.6695$); the gap is dominated by the $n{=}1$ $\infty$Bench-Sum flip ($0.212{\to}0.000$; excluding it $v2$ leads $0.008$). Retrieval on the LME baseline track rises recall@10 $0.767{\to}0.847$ and nDCG@1 $0.720{\to}0.800$.

\subsection{Retrieval diagnostics: channel equivalence bound, gate, budget}
\label{sec:provenance-ablation}

\textbf{Channel ablation (X7, offline).} The derived overlay's retrieval contribution is measured on the 210-question development/gate set with frozen libraries and one factor changed per arm: source-only lexical, $+$semantic, $+$graph-neighbor, $+$relation (full five channels). Recall-any@10 is 72.38/72.38/71.43/72.38\% across arms---every channel effect within \XSevenBound{} at 95\% (percentile bootstrap, 2{,}000 resamples, CIs fully overlapping); recomputed recall@1, MRR, and nDCG@10 agree (\XSevenMetrics{}; per-arm CIs and payload sizes in \cref{tab:provenance-ablation}, appendix). The honest reading is an equivalence bound: on this slice the derived channels do not add offline retrieval recall, and the null speaks only to offline retrieval---in the sandbox path the overlay's online role (scope materialization, versioning, deduplication) is \emph{exercised}, not ablated.

\textbf{Evidence gate.} Enforcement is proved deterministically, without any LLM: an identifier that exists in the scope but was never surfaced by a read tool is rejected with the gate ON and accepted after the one-flag relaxation. Deployed runs: \GateRejectStats{} (\cref{tab:provenance-ablation}, appendix).

\textbf{Budget frontier.} An earlier depth-10-versus-depth-20 replay is retained as an integrity finding: its prefix invariant failed on all 210 questions. The repaired design retrieves once per query at maximum depth and slices a fixed ranking to $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$---prefix monotonicity holds by construction, and the frontier rises from $k{=}1$ to $k{=}20$ with bootstrap CIs (\cref{app:budget}).

\subsection{Integrity and negative results}
\label{sec:negatives}

\textbf{Negative results, stated plainly.} (i) TTL is the structural gap (46 vs.\ published full-context 53.9--96 on MCC tasks): full-context agents keep labeled in-context examples verbatim while any memory system compresses them, mirroring the official finding that full-context models win TTL and LRU; the lever---denser atomic-fact extraction---is future work. (ii) MEME deletion is 0\% (100 episodes, 694 after-task checks)---architectural: the memory model keeps every observation addressable and implements no deletion; until a query-time suppression filter exists, negative-memory semantics (cascade 46.34, absence 59.23 real accuracy) remain central limitations. (iii) Identical Qwen3.7 LoCoMo-1540 answers move 92.21/93.18/93.57 under prompt and judge substitutions, and substituting kimi-k3 as answerer under the same protocol gives 95.19 (cross-judge agreement 98.51\%), so every table labels answerer, judge, and prompt provenance. (iv) Residual failures: the sandbox track's macro-averaged Overall of 0.6511 leaves a 34.9\% gap in Overall terms, concentrated in 117 zero-score questions of 767 (15.3\% micro-average); their recomputed taxonomy is \FailureTaxonomy{}, and the largest single LRU contributor is the $n{=}1$ Summ flip ($0.212{\to}0.000$), disclosed wherever Overall $v1{\to}v2$ is compared---the full non-sampled benchmark run is the right instrument for that domain and has not been run.
