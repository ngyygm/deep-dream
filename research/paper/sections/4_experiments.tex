\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup and comparability}

We evaluate the system on full local runs for LoCoMo, LongMemEval-S, and LoCoMo-Plus, and on a 100-episode MEME filler32k run. The original LoCoMo track contains 1,986 questions and uses token-F1. The Mem0-compatible track contains 1,540 questions after excluding adversarial items and uses binary LLM judging. LongMemEval-S contains 500 questions. LoCoMo-Plus contains 401 cognitive questions. MEME reports heterogeneous task checks over 100 episodes rather than a single 100-question accuracy.

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} keeps these metrics and roles separate. The 1,540-question result uses the exact prompt source from the Mem0 benchmark repository, with Qwen3.7-plus as answerer/primary judge and a Kimi-K3 cross-judge. The LongMemEval and LoCoMo-Plus results use Qwen3.7-plus as answerer and cross-judge under official-compatible prompts. These are protocol-aligned internal results, not reproductions of the published Mem0 or Zep systems.

\subsection{Main benchmark results}

On the original LoCoMo metric, the deterministic quality-v1 baseline reaches 64.99 token-F1 over 1,986 questions. The direct Agent track reaches 93.18\% on the 1,540-question Mem0-compatible binary judge under Qwen3.7-plus and 93.57\% under Kimi-K3. Broken into Mem0's four LoCoMo categories, the Qwen3.7-plus result is 95.36\% single-hop (841 questions), 91.84\% multi-hop (282), 73.96\% open-domain (96), and 94.39\% temporal (321); the Kimi-K3 cross-judge gives 96.08\%/90.78\%/76.04\%/94.70\% on the same populations. The local \texttt{by\_type} codes map to Mem0's categories by question count (type-4=Single-hop, type-1=Multi-hop, type-3=Open-domain, type-2=Temporal), so the per-category breakdown is on identical question populations to Mem0's Table~1. The same full-500 LongMemEval-S run reaches 84.60\%, with category scores of 91.03\% for knowledge updates, 74.44\% for multi-session reasoning, 100\% for single-session assistant questions, 53.33\% for single-session preferences, 98.57\% for single-session user questions, and 84.21\% for temporal reasoning---the same six categories Zep reports on LongMemEval. LoCoMo-Plus cognitive accuracy is 64.59\% under the Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge.

\textbf{K3 as both answerer and judge.} To place a result directly into Mem0's $J$ column under the \emph{same} metric, we run the 1,540-question protocol with Kimi-K3 substituted for GPT-4o-mini as both answerer and judge (the only substitution is the model; the prompt, dataset, library, and CORRECT/WRONG judge are Mem0's). This single run reaches 95.19\% (1466/1540) under the same-model K3 judge---97.38\% single-hop, 93.62\% multi-hop, 80.21\% open-domain, 95.33\% temporal. An independent Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge on the \emph{same} K3 answers scores 94.22\% (1451/1540; 96.91/92.91/77.08/93.46), with cross-judge agreement of 98.51\% (both-correct 1447, both-wrong 70, 23 single-judge-only flips). The two judges therefore agree on all but 23 of 1,540 items, so the 95.19\% is not an artifact of a lenient same-model judge. This $J$ is methodologically the same binary CORRECT/WRONG accuracy as Mem0's published $J$; the two admitted differences are (i) a single run versus Mem0's mean$\pm$std over 10 runs, and (ii) Kimi-K3 replacing GPT-4o-mini as answerer and judge. We disclose one integrity caveat honestly: the run is flagged \texttt{formal\_claim\_allowed=false} because 1,529 historical answer rows predate runtime-code fingerprinting in the legacy source run (the answers themselves are unchanged, answer SHA 76af5772, and the per-item JSONL carries \texttt{runtime\_code\_sha256} on all 2,063 rows). We therefore report it as a diagnostic cross-judge result, not a fingerprint-complete formal result, and it is the number placed in Table~\ref{tab:mem0-baselines} beside Mem0's published $J$.

These values support broad protocol coverage, not a universal ranking. Mem0's paper reports a binary $J$ score on LoCoMo categories, while Zep reports GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini accuracy on DMR and LongMemEval. Because the K3-answerer $J$ shares Mem0's metric but not its model (single run, K3 for GPT-4o-mini, diagnostic-fingerprint caveat), and the LongMemEval/DMR rows use different answerers, we do not subtract these numbers or claim that Deep-Dream outperforms either system.

\subsection{Comparison with published baselines}
\label{sec:published-baselines}

Following the convention of the systems we build on, we place the published results from \citet{chhikara2025mem0} and \citet{rasmussen2025zep} beside our own at matched granularity rather than re-running their systems under our protocol. These tables reproduce the published numbers verbatim; they are cited directly as cross-protocol contextual references, not as a shared leaderboard, and the captions mark the answerer, judge, and metric for each row.

\textbf{LoCoMo (Table~\ref{tab:mem0-baselines}).} \citet{chhikara2025mem0} report F1, BLEU-1, and a binary $J$ (GPT-4o-mini labels each answer CORRECT or WRONG; the score is the percent judged CORRECT, reported as mean$\pm$std over 10 runs) over the four LoCoMo categories for eleven systems (their Table~1). The question populations per category match ours exactly (841/282/96/321), so the category granularity is directly comparable, and the metric is now the same binary $J$: our K3-as-answerer row applies Mem0's CORRECT/WRONG judge with Kimi-K3 substituted for GPT-4o-mini as both answerer and judge. It reaches 95.19\% aggregate (97.38/93.62/80.21/95.33 S/M/O/T) under the same-model K3 judge and 94.22\% (96.91/92.91/77.08/93.46) under an independent Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge on the same K3 answers, with cross-judge agreement 98.51\%. The two admitted differences from Mem0's published $J$ are a single run versus their 10-run mean$\pm$std, and K3 replacing GPT-4o-mini; the run is additionally flagged \texttt{formal\_claim\_allowed=false} (legacy-fingerprint diagnostic, Section~\ref{sec:appendix-protocol}). On weighted $J$, the published Mem0 is $\approx$62.14 and Mem0$^g$ $\approx$61.36. We read our per-category $J$ as same-population, same-metric context, and flag open-domain---our weakest category at 80.21\%---as the slice where Mem0's $J$ likewise sits below its other categories (72.93 for Mem0).

\subsection{Retrieval channel diagnostic}

On a 210-question development/gate set, lexical, semantic, and fused retrieval obtain recall-all of 75.24\%, 70.48\%, and 78.10\%, respectively. Fused retrieval has one more recall-any success and six more recall-all successes than lexical retrieval in this slice. We report this as a diagnostic observation: the set is not held out, no paired confidence interval is computed, and the result does not establish general reliability or final QA improvement.

\subsection{Source-span expansion diagnostic}

We analyze 105 cases selected from prior errors. The controlled baseline is lexical+semantic span retrieval (70.48\% recall-any, 52.38\% recall-all, 3,089 mean response bytes). Holding those channels fixed, adding $\pm1$ turns gives 84.76\%/65.71\%, and adding $\pm2$ turns gives 89.52\%/73.33\%, with 3,609 mean bytes. The $\pm2$ configuration is therefore associated with +19.05 recall-any, +20.95 recall-all, and +16.84\% payload. Because the cases were selected from prior errors and final QA was not rerun on a held-out set, this is a debugging diagnostic rather than a benchmark improvement claim.

\subsection{Integrity findings and the corrected budget frontier}
\label{sec:corrected-budget}

An earlier depth-10 versus depth-20 replay is not a valid budget ablation: its expected primary-result prefix invariant is violated on all 210 questions, because retrieval at limit~10 and limit~20 returns non-monotonic result sets. We retain this failure in the evidence ledger (Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics}, \texttt{retained\_invalid\_replay}) and exclude it from positive figures.

We repair it with a prefix-monotonic depth sweep. Retrieval is run once per reconstructed agent query at maximum depth ($k_{\max}{=}20$, OR-fused by best rank per turn), then sliced to $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$. Because every $k$-slice is a prefix of one fixed ranking, prefix monotonicity holds by construction, with zero violations on all 210 questions. Evidence-payload bytes are measured offline as the UTF-8 size of the \texttt{[turn\_id] text} evidence lines the agent actually consumes; this is the retrieval budget, not the LLM answer bytes. Paired uncertainty is reported as a percentile bootstrap over questions (2{,}000 resamples, 95\%). The resulting frontier rises from $k{=}1$ to $k{=}20$; recall-any $[\text{CI}]$ and recall-all $[\text{CI}]$ are given per~$k$ in Table~\ref{tab:diagnostics}.

MEME exposes a separate weakness. The filler32k run contains 100 episodes and 694 heterogeneous after-task checks; its raw after accuracy is 62.25\% (432/694). Task-level raw/real scores are ER 100\%, Agg 80\%, Tr 97\%, Del 0\%, Cas real 46.34\%, and Abs real 59.23\%. The zero deletion score and weaker cascade/absence scores prevent us from claiming reliable deletion or negative-memory semantics. We report them as central limitations rather than hiding them behind the aggregate.

\subsection{Judge and protocol sensitivity}

The same LoCoMo-1540 outputs score 92.21\% with an older abbreviated judge prompt and 93.18\% with the Mem0 exact prompt. A Kimi-K3 cross-judge yields 93.57\%. Substituting K3 as both answerer and judge on the same protocol yields 95.19\% (single run), with an independent Qwen3.7-plus cross-judge on those same K3 answers scoring 94.22\% (98.51\% cross-judge agreement). The prompt/model changes alter the aggregate by less than two points here, but they also demonstrate why cross-paper numbers need role and prompt labels. We do not interpret the K3-vs-Qwen gap as model strength; a future analysis should report per-question flips, cluster bootstrap intervals, 10-run mean$\pm$std, and independent answer--evidence consistency.

\subsection{Provenance ablation: retrieval channels and the evidence gate}
\label{sec:provenance-ablation}

The X7 ablation isolates the two mechanisms the paper claims matter: the derived-memory overlay (concepts, relations, graph neighborhoods) and the evidence-gate that forces answers back to surfaced source spans. Six arms are run on the 210-question gate set with the same frozen libraries, the same reconstructed agent queries, and the same embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2); only one factor changes per step. The four retrieval arms form a clean channel ablation: arm~1 is source-only lexical (raw-document + episode-bm25), arm~2 adds semantic-provenance, arm~3 adds graph-neighbor, and arm~4 adds relation-evidence to reach the full five channels. Arms~5 and~6 reuse arm~4's ranking and differ only in the evidence-gate: arm~5 keeps the gate ON (the submitted answer may only cite turns the agent surfaced via a read tool), while arm~6 relaxes it so an answer may consume derived memory the agent never read.

Table~\ref{tab:provenance-ablation} reports the offline retrieval replay with 95\% percentile bootstrap CIs (2{,}000 resamples). The retrieval-channel ablation is the honest negative finding of this section: on the 210-question gate set, adding the semantic-provenance, graph-neighbor, and relation-evidence channels moves recall-any@10 by at most one question in either direction (arm~1 = arm~2 = 72.38\%, arm~3 = 71.43\%, arm~4 = 72.38\%), and every arm's confidence interval overlaps every other's completely. The same holds for recall-all@10 (51.43\%--51.90\%, CIs overlapping) and across the full $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$ frontier. The overlay channels therefore do not separate on retrieval recall in this slice. The evidence-payload bytes also barely move across arms (1{,}833--1{,}847 bytes at $k{=}10$), so the overlay does not buy a cheaper ranking either.

The offline part does prove the gate's plumbing independently of any LLM. We pick a turn that exists in a scope but was never surfaced by a read tool and attempt to submit it as evidence. With the gate ON, the submission raises (``Evidence was not surfaced by tools''); with the gate relaxed via \texttt{allow\_unsurfaced\_evidence}, the identical submission is accepted. This is a deterministic, LLM-free proof that the surfaced-set boundary is enforced at submission time and can be relaxed by exactly one flag.

The ablation as reported here is incomplete by design. Arms~5 and~6 are retrieval-identical to arm~4 by construction, so the \emph{retrieval} columns in Table~\ref{tab:provenance-ablation} cannot distinguish them; their separating columns are the agent-level ones---QA accuracy, unsupported-answer rate, and mean evidence tokens consumed by the answerer---which require the six-arm LLM run. The null retrieval result reframes the paper's claim rather than abolishing it: the contribution of the derived overlay is not better retrieval recall on this gate set but a navigable provenance layer and a gate that is actually enforced. Whether that translates into fewer unsupported answers is precisely what the pending arm~5-versus-arm~6 comparison measures, and the acceptance contract keeps the corresponding superiority claim behind that gate.

\subsection{Required controlled experiments}

The current paper is a reproducible system draft, not a controlled SOTA study. The X7 retrieval-channel ablation (Section~\ref{sec:provenance-ablation}) is run offline and its evidence-gate plumbing is proved, but its arm~5/6 agent columns (QA accuracy, unsupported-answer rate, mean evidence tokens) are pending the six-arm LLM run. Before submission we additionally require (i) the arm~5-versus-arm~6 LLM comparison that those columns enable, with unread-ID, cross-scope, inactive-version, false-premise, and answer--evidence tests; (ii) a current/as-of/ever/never update, conflict, retraction, and deletion microbenchmark. The cross-system comparison that an earlier draft listed as item~(iii)---a same-answerer, same-judge, same-prompt Mem0 and Graphiti re-run---is instead discharged by direct citation: Section~\ref{sec:published-baselines} reproduces the published LoCoMo, DMR, and LongMemEval tables from \citet{chhikara2025mem0} and \citet{rasmussen2025zep} verbatim, labeled as cross-protocol contextual references rather than a shared leaderboard. The one exception is Deep-Dream's own $J$ row in Table~\ref{tab:mem0-baselines}, which is computed under Mem0's exact binary-judge protocol with Kimi-K3 substituted for GPT-4o-mini as both answerer and judge (single run, 95.19\%; diagnostic-fingerprint caveat disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:appendix-protocol}); this is the protocol-aware resolution: rather than claim a comparison our answerer/judge/prompt cannot support, we report our number under Mem0's labeled protocol and place the published numbers beside it at matched category granularity, with no subtraction or ranking. A stricter controlled re-run (freezing answer model, judge, prompt, and implementation commit) remains the only path to a shared leaderboard, but it is no longer a submission hard gate. The repaired budget curve (Section~\ref{sec:corrected-budget}) satisfies the budget gate; the acceptance contract makes the remaining items hard gates or requires removing the corresponding headline claim.
