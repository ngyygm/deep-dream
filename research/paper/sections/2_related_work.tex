\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Memory benchmarks.}
LoCoMo and LongMemEval test long conversations, temporal updates, and multi-session reasoning \citep{maharana2024locomo,wu2025longmemeval}. MEME adds deletion, cascade, and absence checks \citep{jung2026meme}. MemoryAgentBench (MAB) evaluates retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding, and selective forgetting in one official scoring framework \citep{hu2025memoryagentbench}; we use it as the primary instrument and keep its task-level metrics visible.

\paragraph{Structured memory.}
MemGPT, A-MEM, Mem0, HippoRAG-2, Cognee, MIRIX, and Zep/Graphiti improve persistence through summaries, notes, or graphs \citep{packer2023memgpt,xu2025amem,chhikara2025mem0,gutierrez2025hipporag2,markovic2025cognee,wang2025mirix,rasmussen2025zep}. Deep-Dream shares the need for compact search but separates search records from answer evidence: a derived node points to a source span; it is not itself answer evidence.

\paragraph{Memory as an addressed store.}
A systems line reads the model stack through computer-architecture lenses: MemGPT casts context management as operating-system paging \citep{packer2023memgpt}, and the ICAM framework posits a context budget---finite windows under attention decay admit only an effective working set \citep{lin2026icam}. These accounts govern the processor side; Deep-Dream supplies the storage side: a persistent store whose address space the agent navigates, where concept families are names, relation and version links are indirection, and source spans are what loading returns. The trade is measurable in our runs: under one model and scorer, loading the whole corpus scores 78.7 where it fits at all (614 of 1{,}074 questions) against 70.8 for addressed reading, and beyond that budget only addressed reading has a score.

\paragraph{Information-theoretic and associative memory.}
The information bottleneck formalizes a compact representation that preserves information relevant to a target variable \citep{tishby2000information}, while rate--distortion theory characterizes the cost of lossy reconstruction \citep{shannon1959coding}. Associative-memory models explain content-addressable retrieval from partial cues \citep{ramsauer2021hopfield}, and probabilistic record linkage separates false-link and missed-link errors \citep{fellegi1969theory}. We use these ideas as an analysis of the memory interface: the graph is a small search code, exact spans are the retained evidence channel, and delayed entity merges trade a tolerable missed-link cost against catastrophic false-link contamination.

\paragraph{Retrieval, long context, and source tracking.}
Retrieval-augmented generation selects evidence \citep{lewis2020rag}. Agentic retrieval instead exposes the retrieval decision to the model: A-RAG hands keyword, semantic, and chunk-reading tools to a reasoning model over open-domain multi-hop corpora and reports accuracy that scales with test-time compute at comparable or fewer retrieved tokens \citep{du2026arag}; its manual failure analysis identifies entity confusion as the dominant residual error, and dialogue and long-form memory remain outside its tested setting. Deep-Dream shares the progressive-disclosure principle but applies it to long-term memory rather than static corpora: concept and version search links, append-only observations, and source-span authority make later reads verifiable rather than merely cheaper, and entity confusion is the failure class that our alignment layer and delayed merges are designed to reduce. Long-context models can lose information when relevant spans are far from the attention window's ends \citep{liu2024lost}. Attribution work commonly checks citations after generation \citep{gao2023alce}; our citation check only verifies that the cited source was opened. It does not prove entailment, and we report that limitation explicitly.
