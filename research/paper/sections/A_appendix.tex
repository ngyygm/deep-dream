\subsection{Artifact ledger}
\label{app:ledger}

The machine-readable ledger is generated by the released ledger and recomputation scripts. It records the source summary, dataset, and per-item JSONL SHA-256 values for every run the paper cites, and is regenerated and compared byte-for-byte with the script output before paper writing. The current ledger covers the MemoryAgentBench tracks (two engine generations $\times$ three answer paths, official scorer at commit 455306d, identical \texttt{entity2id} fingerprint), LongMemEval complete-write pairs and the 500-question run, LoCoMo-1986/1540 and the K3-as-answerer diagnostic, LoCoMo-Plus-401 \citep{li-etal-2026-locomo}, the X7 channel diagnostic, the source-expansion diagnostic, the repaired budget-frontier replay, MEME, BigCodeBench \citep{zhuo2024bigcodebench}, and ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2021alfworld}. Per-run manifests record dataset and prompt provenance, model identifiers, tool observations, evidence IDs, latency, exit status, and code/runtime hashes; failed integrity checks and negative results are retained rather than dropped. The ledger-generation and recompute scripts ship with the released supplementary material so the chain is regenerable end to end.

The released supplementary material contains the machine-readable manifests, per-item scores, prompts, and runtime hashes needed to regenerate every table. The paper reports dataset, model, judge, subset, and integrity status alongside each result so that the comparisons remain auditable without exposing machine-specific storage locations.

\subsection{Version provenance and alignment A/B study}
\label{app:alignment-ab}

The accompanying evidence package adds a complete three-arm alignment study on LongMemEval-S: three document groups (42+49+46 documents, 137 documents per arm), write all documents first, and the same Kimi-K3 endpoint. Arm A is the baseline without batch candidate review; B1 enables batch candidate review; B2 additionally enables the revised convergence implementation (parallel alignment, deferred non-destructive merges, and a final consistency check). The saved B1 and B2 service configurations are byte-identical; their difference is in the runtime code and the B2 switch, so the arm assignment is read from the manifest and code snapshot rather than inferred from filenames. The study is powered for efficiency and consolidation, not quality: each document group contributes only one question per evaluation track.

The separate LME Base/Align+ engine diff (initial/revised implementation) is a post-hoc reconstruction from commits $73d9c5e$ to $8fa9651$. The latter also contains four throughput fixes, and therefore cannot by itself establish which fixes were active at each point in the Align+ run. We treat the manifest-embedded pipeline configuration, runtime-policy hash, and skill hash as authoritative; the reconstructed diff is provenance for the uncommitted code, not a claim that every later fix was active from the first revised run.

\subsection{MemoryAgentBench sampling frame and contamination note}
\label{app:sampling}

The sampled subset was drawn with a fixed task-group list recorded in the released ledger: a 767-question paired sample over 10 task groups and 59 documents, extended in place on the Align+ engine with four task groups (RULER-421K multi-hop QA, 100 questions; the full Redial recommendation corpus, 200 questions; one further DetectiveQA task group, 6 questions; one further $\infty$Bench-Sum task group, 1 question) to 1{,}074 questions over 14 task groups, against the full 3{,}671 questions over 29 tasks. All four competencies are covered; 17 tasks remain unsampled after the extension. The paired sample and its \texttt{entity2id} fingerprint are shared by all six runs (three paths $\times$ two generations); the 307 extension questions exist on Align+ only, and the two large extension document groups (1.9M and 5.7M characters) exceed the full-context reference's strict budget of 1{,}042{,}576 characters, so reference coverage is 614 of the 1{,}074 questions. The reference uses the neutral prompt profile; the normalized-v1 profile of the same run scores 46.0 on its own 607-question coverage, a prompt-sensitivity artifact retained in the ledger and not used as a reference. Document-group lengths are heterogeneous and disclosed wherever cross-system cells lead: the two FactConsolidation document groups are the 6K variants (26{,}157 characters each); the 32K/64K/262K FC variants (136k--1.1M characters) and the other unsampled document groups were dropped for budget, all listed with context sizes in the released ledger's subset block; EventQA is sampled at the 64K/128K/full-book document groups rather than only the published full-book average; and the LRU domain carries $n{=}7$ paired and 14 extended questions ($\infty$Bench-Sum 1 then 2, DetectiveQA 6 then 12), so LRU cells are directional. The MAB source corpora are public; whether the answerer's pretraining saw them cannot be excluded or measured post hoc, and every cross-system table row states its own answerer, judge, and subset.

\subsection{Adjusted Overall derivation}
\label{app:adjusted-overall}

The paired sample omitted two tasks that score low for everyone, AR's MH-QA and TTL's Recommendation, and an earlier draft handled this by imputation: entering published non-full-context reference values (Recommendation $\approx$13, MH-QA $\approx$60) gave an adjusted Overall of $\approx$59.0 against the raw 65.1 at the paired caliber, with a 59--61 range under sensitivity. The extension supersedes the imputation: both tasks are now measured directly on Align+ (source-reading MH-QA 77.0, Recommendation 59.8; \cref{tab:main-results}), and the extended Overall is 71.0 with both tasks inside the aggregation. The extended sample's composition still differs from the full benchmark's (17 tasks remain unsampled), so we continue to state raw values only and never subtract across calibers; the imputed estimate is retained here as provenance for the released ledger.

\subsection{Budget frontier}
\label{app:budget}

An earlier depth-10-versus-depth-20 replay is retained as an integrity finding: its prefix invariant failed on all 210 questions (\texttt{primary\_result\_invariant\_violations=210}), so it is not used for any positive claim. The repaired design runs retrieval once per query at maximum depth and slices a fixed ranking to $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$; prefix monotonicity then holds by construction (zero violations), and the frontier rises monotonically from $k{=}1$ to $k{=}20$ with per-$k$ percentile-bootstrap CIs recomputed from the frozen replay artifacts.

\subsection{An information-theoretic reading of the architecture}
\label{app:info-theory}

This appendix gives the paper's two design commitments a formal shape. Every statement is either a structural property of the architecture (checkable against the schema and code) or a conditional proposition with explicit assumptions; none upgrades the causal wording discipline of \cref{sec:ladder,sec:provenance-ablation}.

\subsubsection{Write time: progressive entity alignment}

Let the immutable source stream be $S=D_1,\ldots,D_T$. Each extracted mention produces a feature vector $X_t$ containing name and content embeddings, attributes, and relation-endpoint co-occurrences. Let $Z$ be the latent entity assignment and let $M_t=(F_t,O_t,G_t)$ be the state after $t$ observations: concept families $F_t$, an append-only observation store $O_t$, and a relation graph $G_t$. An observation is never replaced by a summary; it remains linked to its exact source span.

\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  O_{t+1}&=O_t\cup\{(X_{t+1},\mathrm{span}_{t+1})\},\\
  I(Z;O_{1:t+1})&=I(Z;O_{1:t})+
  I(Z;X_{t+1}\mid O_{1:t})\ge I(Z;O_{1:t}).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:information-growth}
\end{equation}

The second identity is a bookkeeping fact for an append-only stream: new observations can add information about identity, while no prior evidence is discarded. It explains why the system need not solve entity linking on the first mention. A low-confidence merge becomes an additional family (over-splitting); later observations can merge families by redirecting pointers while preserving both observation sets. A premature wrong merge is the dangerous error because it contaminates one search entry with two entities' evidence.

\begin{proposition}[Revisability]
\label{prop:revisability}
In the append-only data model, any coarsening of the current family partition is realizable at a later time by non-destructive merges, with no loss of evidence. If a store instead overwrites an entity with a running summary $\sigma$, then $I(Z;\sigma)\le I(Z;O)$ by the data processing inequality, with strict inequality whenever identity-discriminative detail is discarded.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence under pooled evidence]
\label{prop:convergence}
Assume features are i.i.d. conditional on entity identity and two distinct entities $e\ne e'$ emit distributions $P_e\ne P_{e'}$ with Chernoff information $C(P_e,P_{e'})>0$. For a same/different-entity test using $k$ pooled observations, the likelihood-ratio error obeys $\Pr[\mathrm{error}]\le\exp[-kC(P_e,P_{e'})]$ \citep{chernoff1952measure}. Hence the probability that a true merge, initially split under weak evidence, remains undetected falls exponentially with corroborating evidence.
\end{proposition}

The assumptions are idealized: real windows are correlated, extraction noise is heavy-tailed, and same-name different-entity pairs can have small $C$. The prediction is therefore qualitative rather than a measured convergence rate. It matches the implementation's window-batch adjudication and final consistency check, which re-test candidate families with more pooled context. In the paired engine runs, same-name duplicate groups fall 340$\to$124 (MAB) and 482$\to$99 (LME), while ingest calls per document fall 46--65\%; these are the measured reference values, not estimates of $C$. Relation edges serve two roles: endpoint co-occurrence helps distinguish hypotheses at write time, and the resulting graph supplies additional paths at read time.

\subsubsection{Read time: hierarchical search and step-by-step reading}

The derived layer is a search index, not an answer reconstruction. Let $A_t(q)$ map a question to concept families and relation neighborhoods, and let $\rho$ be an agent policy that chooses which candidates to open. The resulting source contexts form an adaptive chain
\begin{equation}
  C_0(q)\subseteq C_1(q)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq S,\qquad
  a_{k+1}=\rho\bigl(q,M_t,C_{\le k},u_k\bigr),
  \label{eq:progressive-disclosure}
\end{equation}
where $u_k$ is the agent's current uncertainty. Concept search gives a fast first lookup; family observations and relation edges refine it; the final read returns the exact episode and span. This is why the same representation supports both autonomous exploration and source-reading answers.

Define reconstruction distortion as the discrepancy between cited text and the source text it claims to represent, and search loss as failure to place the relevant source span in $C_k$. Since every path resolves candidates to verbatim source spans and the gate accepts only spans surfaced in the current episode, reconstruction distortion is zero by construction; all residual loss is search loss. A summarizing memory fixes a rate--distortion point at write time, whereas $\rho$ chooses the reading budget per query \citep{shannon1959coding}. The agent can stop after a compact concept lookup, or disclose more neighborhoods and source files when uncertainty remains, without changing the authority of the evidence.

\subsubsection{Two-rate decomposition and answer risk}
Let $A(q)$ be the search record emitted by the derived graph index and let $E_C$ be the exact source spans selected by a candidate set $C$. For the answer-relevant variable $Y$, the chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
  I(Y;A,E_C\mid Q=q)=I(Y;A\mid Q=q)+I(Y;E_C\mid A,Q=q).
  \label{eq:appendix-two-rate}
\end{equation}
The first term is the information supplied by the compact concept/relation search record; the second is the information obtained by opening source text. A summary-only memory replaces $S$ by a channel output $\widetilde S=f(S)$ and cannot increase $I(Y;\widetilde S\mid Q)$ beyond $I(Y;S\mid Q)$, whereas Deep-Dream leaves the source channel available after finding candidates. This is an information-bottleneck interpretation rather than a claim that the implementation solves the bottleneck variational problem \citep{tishby2000information}.

For completeness, define $R_q$ as the event that a relevant source span is found and $V_q$ as the event that the verifier answers correctly after reading it. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Pr[\widehat Y=Y\mid q]\ge \Pr[R_q\mid q]\Pr[V_q\mid R_q,q].
  \label{eq:appendix-answer-bound}
\end{equation}
The graph index primarily affects whether relevant text is found, while step-by-step source reading affects verification after the text is opened. The citation check prevents a citation to text that was never opened; it does not guarantee semantic relevance. This factorization explains why improvements should be largest on questions requiring several mentions, temporal updates, or fact consolidation, and why a graph-only offline recall change need not predict the full online gain.

The measured operating points are (27.5k tokens, 0.333), (43.2k, 0.400), and (328.6k, 0.710) Overall on Align+ at the extended sample; the Base generation, frozen at the paired sample, replicates the ordering (0.3025/0.3759/0.6695). The FC-MH scores (2--4 versus 67--70) show where adaptive source reading matters most. X7 bounds the derived channels' offline retrieval contribution at $\pm2.4$ points on the development slice; the graph index's online value is document-group selection and link expansion, exercised by the source-reading agent rather than removed by the ablation.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[One-sided search error]
\label{prop:one-sided}
For any graph-index state, an error in $A_t$ can change which spans enter $C_k$ but cannot change the text or source identifier of a span admitted by the citation check. Thus a search error can miss relevant text, but it cannot fabricate a cited passage; the guarantee is that the cited source was opened, not that it entails the answer.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}

This framing adds mathematical structure without upgrading the causal wording of the ladder: the exponential statement is conditional, duplicate-name counts are a proxy, and the measured ladder remains a systems comparison.
