The machine-readable ledger is generated by the released ledger and recomputation scripts. It records the source summary, dataset, and per-item JSONL SHA-256 values for every run the paper cites, and is regenerated and compared byte-for-byte with the script output at the submission freeze. \subsection{Version provenance and alignment A/B study}

The accompanying evidence package adds a complete three-arm alignment study on LongMemEval-S: three document groups (42+49+46 documents, 137 documents per arm), write all documents first, and the same Kimi-K3 endpoint. The study is powered for efficiency and consolidation, not quality: each document group contributes only one question per evaluation track.

The latter also contains four throughput fixes, and therefore cannot by itself establish which fixes were active at each point in the Align+ run. \subsection{MemoryAgentBench sampling frame and contamination note}
\label{app:sampling}

The sampled subset was drawn with a fixed task-group list recorded in the released ledger: a 767-question paired sample over 10 task groups and 59 documents, extended in place on the Align+ engine with four task groups (RULER-421K multi-hop QA, 100 questions; the full Redial recommendation corpus, 200 questions; one further DetectiveQA task group, 6 questions; one further $\infty$Bench-Sum task group, 1 question) to 1{,}074 questions over 14 task groups, against the full 3{,}671 questions over 29 tasks. The paired sample and its \texttt{entity2id} fingerprint are shared by all six runs (three paths $\times$ two generations); the 307 extension questions exist on Align+ only, and the two large extension document groups (1.9M and 5.7M characters) exceed the full-context reference's strict budget of 1{,}042{,}576 characters, so reference coverage is 614 of the 1{,}074 questions. \subsection{Formal properties of storage and reading}
\label{app:info-theory}

This section makes the storage and reading claims precise. The first result is a structural property of versioned, append-only storage. The second is a conditional statistical statement about delayed alignment. The remaining equations describe the read policy and its answer-risk decomposition.

\subsubsection{Write time: state updates and progressive alignment}

Let $\mathcal D$ be the set of source documents. For $D\in\mathcal D$, let $\mathcal E(D)$ be its Episodes. The $i$th observation is
\[
o_i=(D_i,e_i,\tau_i,x_i,\ell_i),
\qquad e_i\in\mathcal E(D_i),
\]
with processing time $\tau_i$, extracted content $x_i$, and immutable source offset $\ell_i$. Let $O_t=\{o_1,\ldots,o_t\}$ and $M_t=(F_t,O_t,G_t)$ be the state after $t$ observations. The abstract write operator used in the proof is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_{t+1}&=\mathsf{Align}(F_t,x_{t+1}),\\
O_{t+1}&=O_t\cup\{o_{t+1}\},\\
G_{t+1}&=\mathsf{Link}(G_t,F_{t+1},o_{t+1}),\\
M_{t+1}&=(F_{t+1},O_{t+1},G_{t+1}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:app-state-transition}
\end{equation}
The operator $\mathsf{Align}$ may create a candidate family or merge compatible families; $\mathsf{Link}$ adds or redirects pointers. Neither operator changes the source addressed by $\ell_i$. The version chain supplies a projection $\pi_t$ such that $\pi_t(M_{t+1})=M_t$; this is the formal statement that a later state retains the earlier state for audit and revision.

\begin{proposition}[Revisability of delayed alignment]
\label{prop:revisability}
Let $\mathcal P_t$ be the partition of $O_t$ induced by the candidate families in $F_t$. For every coarsening $\mathcal P'_t$ of $\mathcal P_t$, there is a non-destructive pointer map that realizes $\mathcal P'_t$ without changing $O_t$ or any source offset.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A coarsening assigns each old family $f$ to a new family $m(f)$. Keep every observation $o_i=(D_i,e_i,\tau_i,x_i,\ell_i)$ unchanged and replace each family pointer by $m(f)$. Replace an edge $(f,r,o_i)$ in $G_t$ by $(m(f),r,o_i)$. The observation set and every $\ell_i$ are therefore identical before and after the operation, while observations formerly in different blocks can now be reached through the same family. This realizes $\mathcal P'_t$ without destructive rewriting. A split is also safe: it copies pointers into separate candidate families while leaving $O_t$ unchanged. If a system instead stores only a deterministic summary $\sigma=g(O_t)$, then $Z\to O_t\to\sigma$ is a Markov chain for the latent entity assignment $Z$. The data-processing inequality gives $I(Z;\sigma)\le I(Z;O_t)$; the inequality can be strict when $g$ discards information needed to distinguish two identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence with pooled evidence]
\label{prop:convergence}
Assume that, conditional on entity identity, $k$ feature observations are independent. Let two entities $e\ne e'$ have distinguishable densities $p_e$ and $p_{e'}$ with Chernoff coefficient below one. Define their Chernoff coefficient and information by
\[
\rho_{e,e'}=\inf_{0\le s\le 1}
\int p_e(x)^s p_{e'}(x)^{1-s}\,dx,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{Ch}}(e,e')=-\log\rho_{e,e'}>0.
\]
For the equal-prior likelihood-ratio test of $e$ versus $e'$, the Bayes error after $k$ pooled observations satisfies \citep{chernoff1952measure}
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\mathrm{error}]
\le \frac12\,\rho_{e,e'}^k
=\frac12\exp\!\left[-kC_{\mathrm{Ch}}(e,e')\right].
\label{eq:chernoff-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For equal priors, the Bayes error is
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{error}]
=\frac12\int \min\!\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_e(x_j),
\prod_{j=1}^{k}p_{e'}(x_j)\right\}\,d x_{1:k}.
\]
For $0\le s\le1$, $\min\{a,b\}\le a^s b^{1-s}$. Applying this pointwise and using independence gives
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{error}]
\le \frac12\prod_{j=1}^{k}
\int p_e(x_j)^s p_{e'}(x_j)^{1-s}\,dx_j
=\frac12\left(\int p_e(x)^s p_{e'}(x)^{1-s}\,dx\right)^k.
\]
Taking the infimum over $s$ yields \cref{eq:chernoff-bound}. The bound describes an ideal test; real extraction noise and correlated windows determine how closely an implementation approaches it.
\end{proof}

Thus, delayed alignment has a clear qualitative prediction: additional observations can make a correct merge easier to recognize, while an early wrong merge can contaminate one address with two histories. The measured duplicate-family counts (340$\to$124 on MAB and 482$\to$99 on LongMemEval) and ingest-call reductions (46--65\%) are system measurements, not estimates of $C_{\mathrm{Ch}}$.

\subsubsection{Read time: hierarchical search and step-by-step reading}

Let $\mathcal S_t$ be the set of immutable source spans reachable from $M_t$. Given question $q$, set $C_0(q)=\varnothing$ and let
\[
a_{k+1}=\rho\bigl(q,M_t,C_k(q),u_k\bigr)
\in\bigl(\mathcal S_t\setminus C_k(q)\bigr)\cup\{\mathsf{stop}\}.
\]
For a span action, set $C_{k+1}(q)=C_k(q)\cup\{a_{k+1}\}$; for $\mathsf{stop}$, keep $C_{k+1}=C_k$ and terminate. Induction on $k$ proves $\varnothing=C_0(q)\subseteq C_1(q)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal S_t$: the base case is immediate, and the induction step adds either one member of $\mathcal S_t$ or nothing. The equation tracks source-opening actions; index-only lookups update the policy state $u_k$ without changing $C_k$. This is a property of the read protocol; it does not assume that every selected span is relevant.

Let $A(q)$ be the compact concept/relation record used to choose candidates, $E_C$ the exact source spans opened from $C$, and $Y$ the answer-relevant fact. The chain rule is
\begin{align}
I(Y;A,E_C\mid Q=q)
&=H(Y\mid Q=q)-H(Y\mid A,E_C,Q=q)\notag\\
&=\bigl[H(Y\mid Q=q)-H(Y\mid A,Q=q)\bigr]\notag\\
&\quad+\bigl[H(Y\mid A,Q=q)-H(Y\mid A,E_C,Q=q)\bigr]\notag\\
&=I(Y;A\mid Q=q)+I(Y;E_C\mid A,Q=q).
\label{eq:appendix-two-rate}
\end{align}
The first bracket is the information used to locate candidates; the second is the additional information obtained by opening source text. If a summary-only system replaces the source collection $S$ by $\widetilde S=f(S)$, then, conditional on $Q$, the Markov chain $Y\to S\to\widetilde S$ gives $I(Y;\widetilde S\mid Q)\le I(Y;S\mid Q)$ by data processing. Deep-Dream keeps $S$ available after the address layer has narrowed the search; it does not claim to maximize either mutual information term.

For the answer-risk decomposition, let $R_q$ be the event that a relevant source span is in the final opened set $C_K(q)$, and let $V_q=\{\widehat Y=Y\}$ be the event that the answer is correct. The law of total probability gives
\begin{align}
\Pr[V_q\mid q]
&=\Pr[R_q\mid q]\Pr[V_q\mid R_q,q]\notag\\
&\quad+\Pr[\neg R_q\mid q]\Pr[V_q\mid\neg R_q,q]\notag\\
&\ge \Pr[R_q\mid q]\Pr[V_q\mid R_q,q].
\label{eq:appendix-answer-bound}
\end{align}
The first factor is affected by concept and relation navigation; the second is affected by reading and judging the opened evidence. This inequality is a bookkeeping decomposition, not an empirical accuracy bound.

\begin{proposition}[One-sided effect of an address error]
\label{prop:one-sided}
Assume the citation gate accepts a citation $c$ only when $c$ is returned by the current read path and its offset resolves to the immutable source text. An error in $F_t$ or $G_t$ can change which spans enter $C_K(q)$, but cannot change the text or offset of an accepted citation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The gate's acceptance predicate is
\[
\mathsf{Accept}(c)=
\mathbf 1\{c\in C_K(q)\}
\mathbf 1\{\mathsf{resolve}(c)=\mathrm{source}(D,\ell)\}.
\]
The first factor depends on the derived address layer and may be zero for a relevant span. The second factor reads the immutable pair $(D,\ell)$ and is unchanged by edits to $F_t$ or $G_t$. Hence an address error can cause missing evidence, but cannot substitute new text for an accepted source span. The proposition does not guarantee that the accepted span semantically entails the answer.
\end{proof}

The conditional results above explain the design measurements without turning them into unmeasured causal claims: the Chernoff statement is an idealized prediction, duplicate-family counts are a proxy for consolidation, and the benchmark results remain the empirical test of the complete system.
