\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Long-term memory must locate relevant history, keep the wording that makes facts trustworthy, and revise identities when observations arrive. Deep-Dream answers with a document-first temporal concept graph: concepts, relations, and versions form the address layer; append-only observations and source spans form the authority layer; the agent controls evidence expansion.

Source reading helps multi-mention and update questions, relation links connect episodes, and deferred alignment (slow identity decisions) cuts ingestion work; links alone do not guarantee offline recall gains. The broader contribution is the two questions---how to store so that memory accumulates without loss while identity stays revisable, and how to read so that the agent owns addressing---unfolded into the design questions of what is compressed for addressing, what stays authoritative, how identity is revised, and who owns the read budget, together with the memory-hierarchy position: window as working set, store addressed not summarized, load owned by the agent.
