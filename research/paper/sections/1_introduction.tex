\section{Introduction}

A memory answer has at least three evidential layers: what a document says, how the system links that sentence to an entity, and which passages the agent actually reads. Conflating them makes a plausible answer hard to audit. Suppose a document says, \textquotedblleft Alex approved the contract,\textquotedblright and the collection contains an Alex at a laboratory and another at a vendor. The sentence supports an approval claim, but not an identity assignment. Later organization or relation evidence may change the link. A premature merge can make the wrong history appear authoritative.

The same calibration problem occurs with updates. A friend says in January, \textquotedblleft I am avoiding spicy food for now,\textquotedblright and in June, \textquotedblleft I can eat spicy food again.\textquotedblright Both statements are evidence. A current answer should use the later one, while an explanation of the change should inspect both. Retention and interpretation therefore need separate state.

Deep-Dream implements this separation as a \emph{document-first temporal concept graph}. Documents, Episodes, and exact source offsets preserve the record. Observations link extracted content to spans; concepts, relations, versions, and candidate families provide revisable access paths. New evidence can redirect a link without rewriting its source. Processing time orders updates, and dates remain observation content. This is an auditable representation, not a guarantee of correct identity resolution or historical-state recovery.

At query time, the agent follows an address, opens its source passage, and decides whether the evidence is sufficient (Figure~\ref{fig:motivation}). It may search again, inspect a relation neighborhood, or compare earlier and later wording. \textbf{The source gate validates provenance, not identity or entailment.} This distinction keeps a source citation from being mistaken for a proof of the answer.

We evaluate both axes with paired construction runs, MemoryAgentBench read paths, LongMemEval and LoCoMo history tests, and MEME deletion checks. The comparison reports what each protocol establishes: complete paths are not component ablations, duplicate-name counts are proxies, and provenance is not entailment. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Calibrated evidence storage.} Source-linked observations remain inspectable while inferred identity links can be revised.
    \item \textbf{Auditable progressive reading.} The agent can gather additional passages and expose the evidence behind an address.
    \item \textbf{A two-axis evaluation.} We report quality, coverage, costs, alignment uncertainty, null graph recall, and deletion failure with their protocol boundaries.
\end{itemize}
